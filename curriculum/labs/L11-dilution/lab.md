# L11. Dilution: raise enough for the next milestone, not as much as possible

## Goal
After this lab you can decide **how much to raise, at what price and from whom** (investors, grants, customers) so the founders end up with the most money at exit, and you can read a term sheet by its **effective** pre-money, not its headline.

## The principle
**Dilution compounds.** Each round sells a slice of the company, and every slice is taken from what is left. A founder who owns 100% and sells 20% at seed, 20% at Series A and 15% at Series B holds 0.8 × 0.8 × 0.85 ≈ 54% before any option pool. The pool takes more.

**What matters is the founders' money at exit**, not the share today:

> founders' euros = founders' share at exit × exit value

A smaller share of a much bigger company beats a larger share of a small one. Dilution is the price of the money that makes the company worth more; the question is whether each euro bought value worth its price.

**Milestone financing.** Investors value the company by what has been proven. Money raised just before a value-inflection point (a working pilot, a proven production system) is sold at the low, pre-proof price; money raised just after is sold at the high price. So raise enough to reach the next inflection point plus a buffer, and fill as much as possible with money that gives away no shares.

| Round (illustrative, from the sim) | Money | Pre-money | Sold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed | €3M | €9M | 25% |
| Series A | €11M | €40M | 22% |
| Series B | €20M | €100M if the milestone is hit, €40M if missed | 17% / 33% |

## The ideal and the trade-offs
**The goal is not the lowest dilution and not the highest valuation.** It is the highest expected money at exit. Too little money is a cliff; too much is a gentle slope. In the sim, falling short of the milestone cost about a quarter (23%) of the founders' outcome; raising too much cost 1-4%.

**What to aim for:** cost of the next value-inflection point + a buffer of about 20% (roughly 3-6 months of runway), then replace equity with non-dilutive money wherever it is available and not too slow.

**Rules of thumb**
- Plan the round around the milestone, not around the investor's cheque size or the market's mood.
- Do not trade a thin round for a higher headline price: a missed milestone means a down round.
- Compare term sheets by the **effective** pre-money (headline minus the new option pool), not the headline.
- Size the option pool to the hiring plan for the next 12-18 months; negotiate to count it after the money.

| Lever | Effect in the sim | Cost and catch |
|---|---|---|
| **Grant or customer money instead of equity** | Large: €2.5M of grant instead of extra equity lifted founders' money by about 22% | Slow (months of work), uncertain, strings attached (reporting, co-funding). Keep a fallback |
| **Right-sizing the round** (milestone + buffer) | Large when you are short of the milestone (about 23%); small above it (1-4%) | Needs an honest estimate of what the milestone costs |
| **Higher price per euro** | Moderate: a bigger round needed about 35% more price to match a plan-sized one | Valuation is set by what you have proven; a stretch price raises the bar for the next round (down-round risk) |
| **Option pool after the money** | Small-moderate: about +3% for the same 15% pool | Investors usually draft it before the money; it is a negotiation |
| **Pool sized to the plan** | Moderate: 20% instead of 15% cost about 7%; 10% against a 12% need slowed hiring and cost about 4% | A pool that is too small blocks hiring |

## Tests (in the simulation; answers for the teacher)
Setting: two founders with 100%, a seed for a €3M pilot milestone, a Series A (milestone cost about €9M, with buffer about €11M), a Series B, an exit of €400M if the milestone is reached (35% of that if missed). The page shows founders' combined expected money at exit.
1. **The seed round.** Lead investor offers €3M at €9M pre-money (25% sold). Options: A €3M (€108M), **B €1.5M + €2.5M grant (€132M, 1.22×)**, C €4.5M (€99M), D €1.5M without the grant (€93M). Best: B. The instinct (the bigger round) is wrong: €3M leaves no buffer, so Series A is priced from weakness. D is what happens if the grant is not awarded.
2. **How big a Series A.** A €8M at €36M (€102M), **B €11M at €40M (€132M)**, C €14M at €44M (€130M), D €20M at €48M (€127M). Best: B. Short of the milestone costs about 23% (chance of reaching it 65% falls to 38%); too much costs 1-4%. D would need about €54M pre-money to match B.
3. **The option pool.** A 15% before the money (€132M), **B 15% after the money (€135M)**, C 10% before (€127M, below the 12% hiring need), D 20% before (€122M). Best: B (+€3.5M). The carve-out under A is about €3.7M of the €40M headline, so effective pre-money is about €36M. No pool at all: about €119M.

## Evidence
- **Median dilution per round (Carta, US data).** Q1 2024: seed 20.1%, Series A 20.5%, Series B 16.7% (Carta, "Dilution is on the decline"). Q4 2024: seed about 20%, Series B 15% (Carta, State of Private Markets Q4 2024). Over 2025 the median across seed to Series C fell from about 18% to 16%, with Series B falling from about 15% to 12.9% (Carta, State of Private Markets Q4 2025). Dilution has trended down for years, but it still compounds: Carta's own simplified exercise leaves a hypothetical company with about 40% of its shares after a Series D at Q1 2024 medians.
- **What founders keep.** Carta's Founder Ownership Report 2026 (rounds 2021-2025): the median founding team holds about 56% of fully diluted equity at seed and about 36% at Series A. In physical industries the Series A figure is 30.5%, against 37.5% in digital. The median employee pool is 12.1% of equity at seed, and at Series C (16.8%) it exceeds the founders' stake (16.1%).
- **Down rounds are common.** 19% of new investments on Carta in Q4 2024 were down rounds; below 14% in Q4 2025 (Carta quarterly reports).
- **Option pool shuffle.** LTSE's worked example: $8M pre-money, $2M invested. A 20% pool in the pre-money cuts the effective pre-money to $6M and leaves two founders with 60% between them; a 10% pool leaves about 70% (effective pre-money $7M). At a $50M exit each founder gets about $18.75M versus about $19.44M. A pool counted after the money is shared with the investor. The idea goes back to the Venture Hacks essay (not yet verified against the original). That unused options disappear at exit and so give some value back is stated by secondary sources (not yet verified).
- **EIC Accelerator (EU).** The EIC's own page: a lump-sum grant below €2.5M for innovation activities at TRL 6-8, completed within 24 months, and up to €10M of equity (stated as a €1-10M investment component); blended finance combines the two, and at the negotiation stage up to €2M extra can be negotiated. A 2026 work-programme summary from a Hungarian contact point gives the equity range as €0.5-10M and a total Accelerator budget of €634M, so the minimum equity differs between sources (not yet verified; read the official work programme text). EIC grants are aimed at TRL 6-8; at pilot stage a national or smaller EU grant is more likely (not yet verified which apply to the new company).
- **Staging.** Gompers (Journal of Finance, 1995) studied staged venture financing; later papers read it as a way to limit agency problems between investor and entrepreneur (finding taken from secondary descriptions, not yet verified).

## Where it breaks
- **Hot markets.** A big round at a high price can beat the plan-sized round when investors pay a large premium; the sim says about 35% more price makes them even. Take the premium if it is real, but do not let it set the size.
- **Strategic value of investors.** A strong investor, customer-investor or strategic partner can be worth more than the cleanest cap table. Some money buys access, not just cash.
- **Structure beats price.** Liquidation preferences, participation, ratchets and board control can matter more than the valuation. The sim ignores them (not modelled).
- **Grants have costs.** Time, reporting, co-funding and a low hit rate (success rates not yet verified). A grant you do not win leaves a gap.
- **Down rounds are worse than the sim shows.** Underwater options, lost people and slower hiring. The sim includes anti-dilution for the Series A investor but no effect on morale.
- **Not every milestone is clean.** The sim treats the milestone as hit or missed; real programmes slip by degrees.

Answer in under 300 words.

## Takeaways
1. Dilution compounds, and what counts is the founders' money at exit, not the share today. Typical rounds sell about 15-25%; the median founding team holds about 36% by Series A.
2. Raise the cost of the next milestone plus a buffer of about 20%. Too little money is a cliff (missed milestone, down round); too much is a slope (more shares sold before the company is worth more).
3. Replace equity with non-dilutive money where you can: a grant of €2.5M instead of €1.5M extra equity raised the founders' outcome by about 20% in the sim. Grants are slow and uncertain, so keep a fallback.
4. Read the effective pre-money. A pool created before the money comes out of the founders' side; size it to the hiring plan and negotiate to count it after the money.
