## Prompt
A big pilot customer offers a large order if you cut your price by 15%. Your margin is 40%. What do you check first, and what could you offer instead of a price cut? Name one way the discount can hurt you beyond this single deal.
## Model answer
First compute the break-even volume: 15 ÷ (40 − 15) = 60% more units just to earn what you earn now. If the order is smaller than that, accept it only by losing profit. Instead trade something specific for something specific: a longer contract, a volume commitment, a reference case, or a service you have costed, while holding the list price. The wider harm is leakage: other customers hear of the lower price and it becomes the new reference price for everyone.
## Grading notes
Full marks need the break-even number (about 60%), at least one concrete non-price trade with a counterpart commitment, and the leakage or reference-price risk. Accepting the deal "for the volume" without the break-even is a 1.
