## Prompt
Your product saves a customer about €5,000 a year. It costs you €1,900 to make and carry (full cost) and a rival's weaker product sells for €1,800. Compare pricing it cost-plus, at the rival's price and from the customer's value. Which would you start from, and what would stop you charging nearly the whole €5,000?
## Model answer
Cost-plus ignores what the product is worth and can price far below the customer's gain. Matching the rival copies the price of a weaker product and may not cover your fixed costs. Value-based pricing starts from the customer's economic value (the best alternative's price plus what your product adds) and sets the price between your cost (the floor) and that value (the ceiling). Taking nearly all of the gain leaves the customer too little payback, so adoption collapses; a price that shares a part of it, tested with pilots, usually earns the most.
## Grading notes
Full marks need all three methods contrasted, the floor/ceiling logic, and the adoption trade-off (price too near the customer's gain loses buyers). Saying "charge what they will pay" without the mechanism is a 1-2. Exact numbers are not needed.
