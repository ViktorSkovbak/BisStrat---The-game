## Prompt
A large strategic buyer phones you and says it would like to buy your company. You are tempted to negotiate with them alone, because you have a good relationship. Using the winner's curse, what should you do instead, and what are the costs and limits of that?

## Model answer
Bring in more bidders. The highest of several noisy valuations is on average well above the typical one, and a single buyer has no reason to bid up. In the lab's model the expected price rose from about 8.5 (inbound offer) to 9.7 with one more bidder, to 10.3 with three and 11.5 with eight, and a second bidder was worth more than hard haggling with one buyer (Bulow & Klemperer 1996: one extra bidder beats negotiating skill). Costs and limits: banker fees, leaks, management distraction, deals that fail; and sophisticated buyers shade more when rivals multiply, so the real gain is smaller than the lab shows.

## Grading notes
Needs the mirror-image mechanism (max of noisy bids), the action (invite more bidders, including the strategic), and at least one real limit (cost of process, buyers adjust, only works when the value is truly uncertain and common). Rough size (tens of percent, not a few percent) for the top mark.
