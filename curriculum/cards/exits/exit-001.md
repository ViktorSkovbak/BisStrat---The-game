## Prompt
Six companies bid for the same supplier. Each has done its own valuation, and each valuation is uncertain by about 25%. Explain in your own words why the company that wins the auction by bidding its own estimate is likely to lose money, even though no single valuation was biased.

## Model answer
The prize has one true value, but each bidder's estimate is true value plus a random error. The highest bid comes from the highest estimate, and the highest of several noisy estimates is on average an overestimate. So winning is itself bad news about your own estimate: you win exactly when your error is large and positive. Bidding your honest estimate therefore pays more than the prize is worth, on average, even though every estimate was unbiased before the auction. This is the winner's curse (Capen, Clapp & Campbell 1971, oil leases).

## Grading notes
Full marks need: one true value, noisy independent estimates, the winner is the highest estimate (selection), so the average winner overestimates. Saying only "people overbid because of emotion" is a 1-2: the curse happens to rational bidders who ignore selection.
