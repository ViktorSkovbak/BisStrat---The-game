## Prompt
A banker invites you into a broad auction for a company you want to buy. You think it is worth about 20% more to you than to others, but your information is thin. List three moves, other than "bid lower", that improve your expected result, and say which is biggest and why.

## Model answer
(1) Reduce the field: make a pre-emptive offer, join a narrow second round, or find an off-market deal. This is biggest: in the lab, 2 bidders instead of 10 raised expected gain about 40 times. (2) Improve your own information with deeper diligence: it narrows your error and let the lab buyer bid closer, about 4 times better than the broad auction, but it does not change the field. (3) Walk away if you have no real edge: not winning is a fine result, and winning most of the auctions you enter is a warning sign. The edge (20% synergy) is the only lasting source of profit, so check it is real before you bid.

## Grading notes
Needs at least three real levers (fewer bidders, information, walk away or verify the edge) and a reasoned pick of the biggest. Pure "shade more" is not one of the three. A 4 notes the seller must have a reason to accept a pre-emptive offer.
