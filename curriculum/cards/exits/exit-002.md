## Prompt
You are bidding for a company whose value is hard to judge. The banker says the process will have 10 bidders, not the 2 you expected. Does your safe bid go up or down, by roughly how much, and what happens to how often you win and how much each win earns?

## Model answer
Down. The more bidders, the higher the best of their estimates is above the truth, so you must shade deeper. In the lab's model (estimates ±25% off) the shade at which a win stops being a loss rises from about 8% with 2 bidders to about 27% with 10. You also win far less often (about 49% vs 9% at a 30% shade) and each win earns less (about €2.4m vs €0.3m on a €12m prize), so expected gain per auction falls about 40 times. The same applies to uncertainty: at ±10% the curse nearly disappears.

## Grading notes
Needs the direction (shade more), a rough size (3x or more deeper, not a few points), and both channels (win less often, earn less per win). The exact lab numbers are not required. A 4 mentions that uncertainty works the same way.
