## Prompt
Your plan needs about €1M of outside money because of a 120-day cash cycle. You can change one thing: take 30% deposits, halve your stock, ask suppliers for 60 days instead of 30, or raise price 5%. Which would you rank first and last for shrinking the hole, why, and what does each cost you?

## Model answer
Deposits first (about 3.7× smaller hole in the lab): customers pay before delivery, which attacks the longest term. Halving stock is second (about 2.7×), supplier credit third (about 1.7×), and a 5% price rise last (about 1.5×): it adds profit but also bigger unpaid invoices, so it barely helps the timing. Costs: deposits can lose customers, thin stock risks stock-outs, longer supplier terms may raise unit prices or be refused to a new company, a price rise assumes volume holds.

## Grading notes
Full marks need deposits (or customer pre-payment) first, price last with the reason (a timing problem, not a margin problem), and one real cost per lever. Ranking price first is the common mistake: 2 at best.
