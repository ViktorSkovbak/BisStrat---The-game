## Prompt
A company makes a 16% profit on sales and has about 3 months of sales tied up in stock and unpaid invoices (after supplier credit). Roughly how fast can it grow per month on its own cash, and what do you do if the plan is 10% a month?

## Model answer
Profit alone funds about m / (n − m) = 0.16 / (3.2 − 0.16), roughly 5% a month (around 85% a year). At 10% the plan is about twice what profit can finance, so the gap must be closed by shortening the cash cycle (deposits, less stock, supplier credit), slowing growth, or raising outside money sized to the peak need.

## Grading notes
Full marks need roughly 5% (anything 4-6%) and the idea that the rule compares growth with what profit funds, plus at least two of the three responses. A flat "it can grow 16%" (confusing margin with growth) is a 1.
