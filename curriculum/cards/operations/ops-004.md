## Prompt
Two specialists each have their own queue and each runs at 90%. Alternative: both serve one shared queue at the same 90%. Which waits less, roughly how much, and what does it cost you?

## Model answer
The shared queue waits far less: no one sits idle while the other has a backlog. With Sakasegawa's multi-server approximation the queue wait roughly halves for two servers at 90%, and falls further with more servers. The cost is cross-training: generalists are often slower or less expert per job, which raises service time and its variability.

## Grading notes
Needs "pooled waits less", a rough size, and the cross-training trade-off.
