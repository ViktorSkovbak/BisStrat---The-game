## Prompt
You must cut your week from 55 to 40 hours. Which activity do you cut first and which do you protect, and why? Then name one piece of evidence an investor could cite that better management raises output, with its size and its limits.

## Model answer
Cut your own engineering or sales work first: it changes the fewest people's output. Protect priorities and unblocking (they multiply everyone) and investor and customer time (it keeps demand and cash coming; in the lab's model, dropping it costs about 27% of company output, against about 8% for dropping own work). Cutting every activity equally costs about 11%. Evidence: Bloom et al. (QJE 2013), a randomised experiment on Indian textile plants, found better management practices raised productivity about 17% in the first year. Limits: large textile firms in India, a first-year figure, only the abstract verified here; it shows management practices matter, not that a founder's hours do.

## Grading notes
Needs own work as the first cut, the reason (fewest people affected), the protected items, and the Bloom figure with at least one limit. The modelled percentages are illustrative; do not penalise rounding, but do penalise cutting 1:1s or external time first. Citing Bloom and Van Reenen's correlation as proof of causation is a 2.
