## Prompt
Explain in your own words what loss aversion says and roughly how large the effect is. Give one place in your own company where it will matter.

## Model answer
People judge outcomes as gains or losses from a reference point, and a loss weighs about twice as much as an equal gain (the 1992 estimate is 2.25; pooled later estimates are nearer 2). A €1,000 loss needs about €2,000 of gain to offset it. It matters whenever someone feels they are giving something up: a customer moving from free to paid, a price rise, or you stopping a project you have spent money on.

## Grading notes
Full marks need the reference point, "about 2x" (not "a bit more") and a concrete business case. Saying only "people hate losing" is a 1-2. Quoting 2.25 as an exact constant for everyone is a 3.
