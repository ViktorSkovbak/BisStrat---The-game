## Prompt
A product line has absorbed €2M and is late. Continuing costs €1M more with a 30% chance of a €3.2M return; stopping recovers €0.2M. How should the €2M enter the decision, and what do you do before you spend the money so you decide well later?

## Model answer
It should not enter at all: the €2M is gone whichever way you choose. Compare only what happens next: continuing is -1.0M + 0.3 x 3.2M = -0.04M, stopping is +0.2M, so stop (continuing needs about a 38% chance to match stopping). The pull to continue is loss aversion: stopping makes the loss real, a gamble keeps a chance of zero (escalation of commitment, Staw 1976). Write kill criteria with a date and threshold before spending, and ask "would I fund this today if I inherited it?".

## Grading notes
Needs the forward-only comparison with rough numbers and the break-even chance, plus a pre-commitment device. "Past spending doesn't matter" with no numbers is a 3. Missing that a pivot or continuation can still be right if the future numbers say so is a deduction.
