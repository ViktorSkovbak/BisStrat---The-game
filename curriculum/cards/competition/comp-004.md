## Prompt
A well-funded rival is entering your accounts and will undercut if it pays. Name three levers to avoid a price war, rank them for a hardware startup and give the catch of each. Which one would you avoid?

## Model answer
Differentiate: change the payoffs so a cut wins the rival less than holding high (it must cross a threshold; half-measures did nothing in the lab, about 3x for clear differentiation). Signal a clear public rule to answer cuts: cheap, but it works only if the rival believes you (the lab's rival needed over about 56% belief; in the Perth gasoline study of Byrne and de Roos the shift to coordination took about three years), and talking prices with a rival can break competition law. Win share fast with a price push: avoid it against a rival with deeper pockets, because it starts the war (about 1.2x, and negative without lasting customers). Real example of the cost of a war: the 1992 US airline fare war.

## Grading notes
Full marks: three levers, a ranking with reasons, the catch of each (threshold, belief and time, funding), and the lever to avoid. Answers that only say "differentiate" with no mechanism, or recommend matching the cut to win the account, are 1-2.
