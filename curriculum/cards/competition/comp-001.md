## Prompt
You and one rival sell similar hardware and each quarter set a high or a low price. Explain in your own words why undercutting is the rational move in a one-shot game, but holding the price can be stable when you expect to meet again. What single quantity decides which one wins?

## Model answer
In one round, a cut pays more than holding whatever the rival does (the prisoner's dilemma), though both low is worse for both than both high. When the game repeats, a cut today is answered by a cut tomorrow, so the one-time gain (undercut alone) must be weighed against all the later quarters at the war price. The deciding quantity is the chance the contest goes on, the shadow of the future. With the lab's payoffs (90 both high, 150 undercut alone, 30 both low) holding high beats undercutting against a rival that retaliates once the chance of another quarter is above about 50%.

## Grading notes
Needs the one-shot logic, the retaliation mechanism, and the chance of continuing (or number of future rounds) as the decider. A number near 50% for the lab's payoffs is a bonus; "cooperation is nice" or "trust" without the incentive logic is a 1.
