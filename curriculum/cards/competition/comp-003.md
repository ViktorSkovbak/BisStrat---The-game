## Prompt
Your market will end in two quarters because of a technology switch, and you and your rival have held prices high so far. Should you keep the truce? Explain what happens to cooperation when the end date is known.

## Model answer
No. The truce depends on the threat of retaliation next quarter; with no next quarter a cut costs nothing, and a rival that also sees the end will cut first (in the lab: cut now and stay low about 2x the value of holding the truce; holding was the worst option). The unravelling starts at the last round and works backwards, so expect cuts before the final quarter. Real customers remember and you may meet the rival in another market, so end-games are softer than the model.

## Grading notes
Needs the mechanism (no future to retaliate in, backward unravelling) and a decision (cut, and expect the rival to cut first). Saying "keep cooperating to protect the relationship" without addressing the end is a 1.
