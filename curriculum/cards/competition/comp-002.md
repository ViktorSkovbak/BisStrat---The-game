## Prompt
A rival cuts its price once and you cannot tell whether it is a test or the start of a campaign. Name three possible responses, say which you would pick and why, and name the trap in each of the other two.

## Model answer
Ignore it: works only against a rival that copies you; against one that keeps cutting until it is answered you hand over the customers (in the lab, 0 against that type). Match for good: both of you lock into the war price (about 0.8x the value of doing nothing in the lab). Match once, then go back to high: ends a test without feeding a war, at the cost of one weak quarter (about 1.8x in the lab). This is tit-for-tat made forgiving: Axelrod's winning strategy was nice, retaliatory and forgiving, and strict copying can echo a cut back and forth.

## Grading notes
Full marks: names at least three responses, picks proportionate response plus return to high, gives the trap for each, and links to nice/retaliatory/forgiving. "Always match" or "always ignore" with no trap named is a 1-2.
