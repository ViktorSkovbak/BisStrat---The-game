## Prompt
Your hardware startup is negotiating a first pilot. A smaller buyer would pay you about €600 a unit; the big customer would pay at most about €900, but you cannot see that. Explain what your BATNA, your reservation price and the ZOPA are here, and what happens to your power if you land a second buyer at €700.

## Model answer
Your BATNA is the smaller buyer: what you do if the talks fail. Your reservation price is the price where this deal is exactly as good as that fallback, about €600. The ZOPA is the zone between your reservation price and theirs (about €600-€900), the only range where a deal works; you see only your own edge of it. A second buyer at €700 raises your floor to €700, narrows the zone to €700-€900 and lifts the expected price, because you can now walk away from a bad number.

## Grading notes
Full marks need the three terms tied to the case, and the point that power comes from the alternative, not from argument. Defining BATNA as "a backup plan" without linking it to the reservation price and the floor is a 2-3. Missing that the other side's limit is hidden is a 3.
