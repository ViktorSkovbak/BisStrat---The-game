## Prompt
Your advisor says: "Never name a price first; let procurement show their hand." Explain what the evidence on first offers says, give a rough size, and say when you would follow the advice and when not.

## Model answer
Studies (Galinsky & Mussweiler 2001, and a summary of many anchoring studies) find the opening number pulls the final price toward itself, for buyer or seller: roughly 37 cents of final price per euro of opening number in the summary I used, and correlations of .8-.9 in one set of experiments. So waiting hands them the anchor. Open first when you have prepared: at or a little above what you think they could pay, with reasons, because absurd anchors raise the risk of a walkout. Exceptions: you do not know their range at all (ask questions first), or you have no alternative and a skilled counterpart; and a prepared counterpart who focuses on their own alternatives is largely immune.

## Grading notes
Needs the mechanism (anchoring pulls the outcome), a rough size (about a third to a half of the gap), a clear stance (open first, ambitious but defensible) and one real limit. "Anchoring exists" with no size or rule is a 2. Advising an extreme opening with no mention of walkout risk is a 2.
