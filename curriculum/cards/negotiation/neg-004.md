title: Trade across issues instead of splitting
source: L16

## Prompt
The customer wants €60 a unit off. Payment in 60 days costs them little to shorten, but it matters a lot to your cash. A bigger volume commitment cuts your unit cost. Say what you do, why it beats splitting the difference, and what you check first.

## Model answer
Trade: give the €60 only against payment in 15 days and a larger volume commitment. The two sides value the issues differently (cash is dear for a startup, cheap for them), so the same concession buys more for you than it costs them, and the zone of agreement grows (in the lab, from about €300 to €358 a unit, and the outcome was about 2.8× holding firm). Splitting the difference only divides a fixed price and gave about 1.1×. Giving the price away for nothing was no better than holding firm. Check first whether those terms are really negotiable for them (payment policy, purchasing rules).

## Grading notes
Full marks need the logroll mechanism (different valuations), a clear call, and the check on what is negotiable. "Compromise in the middle" is a 1-2. Missing the "never concede on one issue without asking for another" rule is a 3.
