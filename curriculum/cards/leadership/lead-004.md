## Prompt
You will step back as CEO but want to stay rich and keep a real say. What do you keep, what do you trade, and give one reason Wasserman's finding might mislead you.

## Model answer
Keep the one control you could not live without, for example the board chair with a say on big calls, and trade the rest: an experienced CEO, an investor-led board otherwise, and a strong co-founder only if the value they add outweighs the equity they take (about 12% in the lab). In the lab's model this keeps roughly 80% of the richest route's euros. The finding can mislead because it is an association: strong companies may get better terms and weak ones may be pushed into giving up control, so about 20% per control is an average, not a price list. Competing term sheets or grants can lower the price.

## Grading notes
Needs one specific control kept, the rest traded, and a concrete limit (reverse causality, leverage, terms versus titles, founder-dependent businesses). A generic "it depends" is a 1-2.
