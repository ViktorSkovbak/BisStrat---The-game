## Prompt
Your first hardware unit costs €4,000 and your engineers expect cost to fall 15% every time cumulative output doubles. Roughly what does the 256th unit cost, and about how many units until you reach €1,000? Explain in your own words why cost falls with cumulative output.

## Model answer
Eight doublings (2^8 = 256) at 0.85 each: about 0.27 of the start, so roughly €1,100. To reach a quarter of the start cost you need about 8.5 doublings, which is about 370 units. Cost falls because people find faster methods, designs get simpler, scrap and rework drop, and suppliers improve. It is many small improvements that depend on cumulative output (experience), not on calendar time.

## Grading notes
Full marks need the right order of magnitude (about €1,100 and a few hundred units) and a mechanism that ties cost to experience. Treating the fall as linear in units, or as depending on time alone, is a 1-2. Exact arithmetic is not required.
