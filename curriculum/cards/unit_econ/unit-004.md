## Prompt
Your investors fund 24 months and you build about 28 units a month at an 85% learning rate. You can reach €1,000 from €4,000 after about 370 units. A board member says: "Let's promise €500." What do you answer, and what could make the whole curve fail?

## Model answer
Each halving of cost needs the same number of extra doublings, about 4.3 at 85%, which is about 19× more units: €500 needs roughly 7,100 units, over 20 years at 28 a month, against 13 months for €1,000. Promise €1,000 in this round and treat €500 as a later step that needs a bigger market or a better learning rate. The curve can fail if a rival technology descends a steeper one (Solyndra, whose modules lost out to crystalline silicon in 2011), if redesigns reset learning, or if the learning rate is lower than assumed.

## Grading notes
Full marks need the units arithmetic (roughly 20× per halving), the conversion to months, and one concrete way the curve breaks. A generic "ambition is risky" answer is a 1-2.
