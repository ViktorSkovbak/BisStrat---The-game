## Prompt
You sell pilot units at today's cost of €4,000. Your target cost is €1,000 and your line can build 30 units a month. Where should you price, and why is pricing at the target cost not the textbook best?

## Model answer
Price low enough that demand fills the line, and no lower. A lower price buys volume, volume brings cost down sooner and shortens the months you pay the team. Once demand equals capacity, a further price cut adds no units and only gives away margin. In the lab that is about €1,900-2,000: about 13 months and 2.2× less cash than pricing at €4,000. Pricing at €1,000 needed about €725k, close to twice as much as €2,000. Check the demand response with a few real customers first.

## Grading notes
Full marks need the capacity cap and the cash trade-off, not only "forward pricing wins". Saying "price at target to follow the experience curve" without the cap is a 2. Naming the demand-response assumption as the thing to test earns the top grade.
