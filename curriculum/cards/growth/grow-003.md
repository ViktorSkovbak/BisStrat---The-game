## Prompt
Your Bass forecast gives about 73 units in year 1, a peak of about 330 in year 8 and an average of about 230 over ten years. Your manufacturer wants one capacity number. What do you propose, and what goes wrong with sizing it for the average or exactly for the peak?

## Model answer
Plan in modules timed to the curve, ending at the peak plus slack (roughly 20%), for example 190 a year at launch with two modules of about 105 in years 4 and 6. Sizing for the average (230) loses about 17% of demand and runs flat out from year 5. Sizing exactly for the peak (330) runs at about 99% load in the peak year and above 85% for four years; by Kingman's law waits blow up there. Sizing for peak plus slack from day one works but buys about a fifth more capacity-years, much of it idle for years. Early sales also re-estimate p and q, so each module is a re-forecast point.

## Grading notes
Needs: the peak is about 1.4x the average and late, a staged plan with slack at the peak, and the load/queueing reason for avoiding 100%. "Build for the peak" with no slack or no staging is a 2.
