## Prompt
An investor says "ten strong reference customers will kick-start the S-curve". In a Bass model with 3,000 buyers and p = 0.02, how much does that head start roughly buy, and what is the real argument for references? Name one place the Bass model misleads you here.

## Model answer
Marketing alone already brings about 60 buyers a year (3,000 x 2%), so each early customer is worth only about 5-6 days of that flow: ten references move the curve about two months, about 1.1x customers by year 3; thirty about five months. The real argument is credibility: references make every later buyer easier to win, which acts on q, and q is the larger lever. Where Bass misleads: it treats all adopters as equally persuasive and the market and q as fixed, whereas in B2B a few named references matter far more than average adopters, and a bad one can do real damage.

## Grading notes
Needs the order of magnitude (weeks to a few months, not years) and the q-credibility argument, plus one valid limit (unequal adopters, moving q or market size, supply shortages). "Yes, references kick-start growth" without sizing is a 1.
