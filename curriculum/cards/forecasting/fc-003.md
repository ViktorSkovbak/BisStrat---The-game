## Prompt
In the lab, a forecaster who says 90% or 10% on gut scored worse than one who says 50% on everything, while one who starts from the base rate and adjusts scored best. Explain why, and say what the base-rate forecaster does that the bold one does not.

## Model answer
Bold calls are only worth making if the gut read is sharp enough to back them. Here it is noisy (typical error about 15 points), so the 90% calls happened only about 61% of the time and the 10% calls about 29%; the squared misses cost more than the occasional hit gains. Saying 50% scores 0.25 and never loses big. The base-rate forecaster starts from how often it happens (40%) and moves only as far as her evidence has predicted before, so her numbers are calibrated (Brier about 0.206, about 18% better than a coin flip). Always quoting the base rate scores 0.240: the adjustment is where the skill is.

## Grading notes
Full marks: connects boldness to the noise of the gut read, names calibration (what happened at 90%), and states that the base rate is a start and the adjustment adds the skill. "Because bold people are overconfident" without the mechanism is a 2.
