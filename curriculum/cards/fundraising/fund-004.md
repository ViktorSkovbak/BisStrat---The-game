## Prompt
A term sheet offers a €40M pre-money valuation but requires a 15% option pool created before the investment. What does that do to the price, what do you ask for, and which number do you compare term sheets by?

## Model answer
A pool in the pre-money comes out of the existing shareholders' side only: the investor still gets the same percentage, so the effective pre-money falls (about €36M of the €40M here). This is the option pool shuffle. Ask for the pool to be sized to the hiring plan for the next 12-18 months, and for it to be counted after the money so the investor shares the cost. Compare term sheets by the effective pre-money (headline minus the new pool), not the headline. Do not cut the pool below what hiring needs: the lab's too-small pool slowed hiring and cost more than it saved.

## Grading notes
Full marks need who pays (founders only), the effective-price idea, both asks (size and timing) and the limit (too small blocks hiring). Saying only "negotiate the valuation up" is a 1-2.
