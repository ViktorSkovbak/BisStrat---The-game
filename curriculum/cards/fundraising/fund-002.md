## Prompt
The next milestone of your hardware company costs about €9M. An investor offers €8M at a high valuation, another offers €11M at a slightly lower one, and a third offers €20M. Which do you take and why? Explain why the risk of raising too little differs from the risk of raising too much.

## Model answer
Take the €11M: the cost of the milestone plus a buffer of about 20% (3-6 months of runway). Money raised before the inflection point is sold at the low, pre-proof price, so spare cash is expensive, but the cost is a slope (in the lab, 1-4% of founders' money). Falling short is a cliff: the milestone is missed or delayed, the next round becomes a down round or a bridge at a discount, and the outcome fell by about a quarter. The €8M has the lowest dilution but the lowest chance of reaching the milestone; the €20M only wins if its price is much higher (about 35% in the lab).

## Grading notes
Full marks need the rule (cost plus buffer), the asymmetry (cliff versus slope) and the reason (price rises after the milestone). Choosing the highest valuation or the most money without tying it to the milestone is a 1-2.
