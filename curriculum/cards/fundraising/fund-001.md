## Prompt
Two founders own 100%. They sell 20% at seed, 20% at Series A and 15% at Series B, and create a 10% option pool at seed. Roughly what do they own after Series B, and what number should they maximise instead of their ownership share?

## Model answer
About 48% between them (0.7 after seed with the pool, then 0.8 and 0.85), before any pool top-ups. Dilution compounds because every round sells a slice of what is left. The number to maximise is the founders' money at exit: share at exit times exit value. A smaller share of a much bigger company beats a bigger share of a small one, so each round is worth it only if the money buys more value than the slice it costs.

## Grading notes
Full marks need the compounding (a figure in the 45-55% range) and the exit-euros metric. Adding the rounds (20 + 20 + 15 = 55% sold, 45% kept) is a 2: the direction is right but the mechanism is wrong. Saying "keep as much as possible" is a 1.
