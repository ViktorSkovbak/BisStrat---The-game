## Prompt
A key component is in short supply and your distributors start ordering 40% more than they sell. Your own supplier is rationing. What do you do with the incoming orders and your own component orders, and what happens if you do the opposite?

## Model answer
Do not pad. Check orders against real sell-through, allocate scarce units by past sales (not by order size), tell customers the rule, and order components against sell-through. In the model this cut the swing at the supplier from 36× to 16× (about 2.3×). Padding your component orders by 50% pushed it to 54×. Inflated orders vanish when the shortage ends and everyone is left with stock; chips in 2021 to 2023 showed the pattern.

## Grading notes
Full marks: allocate by history, verify against sell-through, no padding, and the mechanism (phantom orders vanish, leaving a glut). Saying "build a big buffer" is a 1-2; it was worse in the model.
