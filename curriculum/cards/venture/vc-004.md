## Prompt
A partner says your company has a 15% chance of working but a typical success case of €400M, below the fund's bar of about €1B. You can show only one piece of proof in four months: pilots that lift the chance of working to 25%, or customer evidence that the success case is about €800M. Which do you choose, and why?

## Model answer
Show the bigger success case. The chance of returning the fund is the chance of working times the chance the win clears the bar. Pilots multiply only the first term, here by 1.7x (about 2.3% to 3.9%). Evidence of an €800M case moves the whole outcome toward the €1B bar, lifting the chance to about 6% (2.6x). Show the size of the win first; reduce risk after the win clears the bar. Catch: the evidence must be customer data, not a slide, and a plan with a €250M ceiling is not venture-fundable however safe it is.

## Grading notes
Full marks need the product logic (chance of working x chance above the bar), the right pick with the rough sizes (about 1.7x vs about 2.6x), and the caveat about evidence quality or venture fit. Picking lower risk by default is a 1-2.
